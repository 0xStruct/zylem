A verifiable data layer for Zcash

Don’t trust the server. Verify every block with Zylem.

Zylem separates who serves Zcash data from who you trust. History travels as portable Ring files, each about a day of blocks, that anyone can host, and every client checks what it receives before using it. Wallets are the first application.

  • No trusted server: every block is checked on your side
  • Any host can serve the history
  • No full node: Rings verify on their own, and proofs make them light enough for phones

Where Zylem sits

Stronger than any light wallet, far lighter than a full node.

A light wallet trusts its server; a full node checks everything and needs a server's worth of resources. Zylem checks every block you use on your own device, with one small proof standing in for every historical header.

What Zylem doesn't do yet

  • Re-check other people's transactions. Like any light client, it relies on most hashpower enforcing the rules for transactions that never touch you.
  • Validate the transactions that touch you, guard supply and catch double spends. Planned: local proof and signature checks for your own transactions, pool-balance tracking, and a proven nullifier set.
  • Watch the mempool. Payments are verified once they are mined.
  • Protect a fresh install whose every source is eclipsed. Minimum chain work and several sources narrow this; they cannot remove it.

One verified history, many consumers

Who serves the data no longer decides what you trust.

Zylem separates data availability from verification. Anyone can build Rings and proofs, any host can serve them, and every consumer checks them itself. Wallets are the first consumer, not the last.

How Zylem works, in detail

Two ways to verify

Rings work on their own. Proofs make them light.

Every Ring can be checked without a proof. A shared history proof replaces the heavy part, the headers, for clients that cannot afford it.

Full Rings

Working on testnet

Each Ring carries its blocks' headers, so the client checks every header's Equihash, difficulty and link itself, from a checkpoint. No proof system to trust.

  • Best for desktops, servers and the proxy
  • About 1.5 KB of header per block to download and check
  • History checked in order from the checkpoint

Light Rings + history proof

Working on testnet

Rings carry 104-byte block records instead of headers, and one SP1 proof shows every header followed Zcash's rules, Equihash included. The client checks the proof, then the records against it; transactions and note trees are checked as in a full Ring.

  • On testnet: a Ring with its 1,536 proven blocks light drops from 7.19 MB to 5.10 MB and verifies in the browser (demo, step 5). With every block proven, a Ring is about 2 MB
  • One proof of about 0.8 MB instead of about 2.7 GB of headers for mainnet since NU5
  • One check in about 40 ms instead of 1.84 million Equihash checks
  • Jump straight to any Ring; the proof can start at genesis at no cost to clients
  • Small enough for phones, browsers and, wrapped, other chains

Developer quickstart

Try verified Zcash sync without rewriting your wallet.

Point an existing light wallet at the Zylem proxy instead of its usual server. It speaks the same protocol, so the wallet only needs a new server address.

1 Run the proxy with the shipped checkpoint
./target/release/zylem-proxy --rings http://127.0.0.1:8090 \
    --headers-rpc http://user:pass@127.0.0.1:18232/ \
    --upstream https://testnet.zec.rocks:443 \
    --checkpoint "$(jq -r .checkpoint checkpoints/testnet.json)"
2 Sync an existing wallet through it
zcash-devtool wallet -w my-wallet sync --server 127.0.0.1:9067
3 What a rejection looks likeexample output
WARN zylem: REJECT Ring 4518274..=4522369 rejected: transaction 4 at height 4521980: rebuilt ID does not match
WARN zylem_proxy: serving only the verified range up to 4518273

Testnet only, from the Zylem repository. The proxy reads Rings from a host such as zylem-build serve --out data/testnet --rpc http://127.0.0.1:18232/, and zylem-build probe --lwd http://127.0.0.1:9067 shows whether it accepts its current tip.
